Difference Between a Vector Image vs Pixel Image<\/h2>\n\n\n
\n
\n

Vector Image Format:<\/u><\/strong><\/em><\/span><\/h3>\n
    \n
  • It is like a drawing made with lines and mathematical shapes.<\/span><\/li>\n
  • It can be resized larger or smaller without becoming blurry or pixelated.<\/span><\/li>\n
  • It is ideal for logos, drawings, stickers, and things we want to print in different sizes without losing quality.<\/span><\/li>\n
  • The files are usually smaller and easier to edit.<\/span><\/li>\n<\/ul>\n

    Pixel Image Format (Raster):<\/u><\/strong><\/em><\/span><\/h3>\n
      \n
    • It is like a photo made up of small color boxes called pixels.<\/span><\/li>\n
    • If we enlarge it too much, the squares become visible and the image looks blurry.<\/span><\/li>\n
    • It is great for photographs and very detailed design elements.<\/span><\/li>\n
    • The files can be larger, and sometimes it's difficult to edit without losing quality.<\/span><\/li>\n<\/ul>\n

      Summary:<\/u><\/strong><\/em><\/span><\/h3>\n

      In short, vector images are perfect for designs that need to scale and resize without issues, such as logos and stickers, while pixel-based images are better for photos and detailed designs, but they can't be resized too large without losing quality.<\/span><\/p>\n<\/div>\n\n\n\n
